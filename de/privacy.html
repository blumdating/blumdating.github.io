Datenschutzerklärung

Dieses Dokument ist vorerst auf Englisch. Eine geprüfte Übersetzung folgt.

Kurz gesagt

  • We only ask for what Blum needs to help you meet people. We never sell your data and never show ads.
  • Your disability and health answer is optional. We keep it only with your explicit consent, it’s hidden unless you choose to show it, and you can delete it at any time.
  • Other people see your city and a rounded distance, never your exact location.
  • Face verification happens on your phone. We never keep your selfie or any face data.
  • Your messages are only between you and your match, unless one of you reports the other. If one of you taps Translate on a message, only its text is sent to be translated.
  • You can download your data or delete your account at any time in Settings.
  • If you live in the USA, our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy also explains how we handle health data.

Who we are

Blum is run by Afonso Miguel Martins Delgado dos Santos and Duarte Miguel Bandeira Lisboa Bento, Rua da Nascente, 14, Fonte Lima, 2530-728 Santa Bárbara, Lourinhã, Portugal (“we” or “us”). We decide together how your personal data is used, which makes us its joint controllers under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which applies in the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, and the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) for people in Switzerland.

For anything about your privacy, write to blum.dating.app@gmail.com. We’ve agreed between us that this address handles every request about your data, and both of us answer for how it’s used. You can also use your rights with either of us.

If you live in the USA or Canada, the parts “If you live in the USA” and “If you live in Canada” below also apply to you.

Your consent when you sign up

Blum is a place for people with disabilities, little people and those who want to meet them. So your profile, your photos, what you write and the people you choose to see can reveal something about your health, a disability or your sexual orientation. The GDPR protects these as special categories of data.

When you sign up, we ask for your explicit consent to use this information to run Blum: to show your profile, deliver your messages, suggest matches and translate what you write when someone asks. The “I agree” tick is never pre-ticked, and we keep the date you gave it.

We can’t run Blum for you without this consent, so withdrawing it means deleting your account (Settings → Delete account). The separate choices about your health answer and your orientation, below, stay optional.

Legal basis: your explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)).

Your account

Your email address, or your name and email from Google if you sign in with Google, and your date of birth.

We use them to create your account, keep it secure and check that you’re 18 or older. We show your age, never your birthday.

Legal basis: our contract with you.

Your profile

Your first name, age, gender, who you’re interested in, photos, bio, hobbies and conversation themes, and, if you add them, your job, height and what you’re looking for.

Members who can see you on Blum see your profile: that’s how people find each other. Before a photo is saved, we resize and re-save it, which removes hidden details such as where it was taken.

Before other members see a new photo, our own server checks it automatically for nudity, without sending it to any other company. If the check isn’t sure about a photo, it stays hidden until a moderator has looked at it; clearly explicit photos are refused.

Legal basis: our contract with you, and, for anything in your profile that reveals your health or your sexual orientation, your explicit consent given when you sign up.

Your disability and health answer

Blum asks whether you have a disability or a health condition, for example dwarfism, or being a wheelchair user. Any answer is health data, which the GDPR protects as a special category of data.

We store it only with your explicit consent: an “I agree” tick that is never pre-ticked, and we keep the date you gave it. “Prefer not to say” stores nothing.

We use it to suggest people who share your experience and to apply the “Show me” and “Who can see me” choices. It stays hidden unless you choose to show it on your profile, and other people’s “Show me” filters only see answers that are shown.

It’s never used for advertising, never included in usage statistics and never sent to our AI provider.

You can delete it at any time in Settings → Privacy, which also withdraws your consent. Deleting it doesn’t affect anything done before.

Legal basis: your explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)).

Your sexual orientation

Adding your sexual orientation is optional. We store it only with your explicit consent: an “I agree” tick that is never pre-ticked, and we keep the date you gave it. It’s shown on your profile, and you can remove it at any time in Edit profile, which also withdraws your consent. Your gender and who you’re interested in can also say something about it; we use them under the consent you give when you sign up.

We use these only to show you the people you asked to see, and to show you to them. Your orientation is never used to order profiles and never sent to our AI provider.

Legal basis: your explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)).

Matching questions

Optional questions about your lifestyle, weekends, children, smoking, drinking, pets and how you like to keep in touch, and how much it matters to you that a partner has a similar disability. Your answers are never shown to anyone.

We use them to suggest people you’re likely to get on with. Your answer about a partner’s disability can say something about your own health, so it’s treated like your health answer: we store it only with its own explicit consent, a tick that is never pre-ticked, and it’s never sent to our AI provider.

Legal basis: our contract with you, and, for the question about a partner’s disability, your explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)). Every question can be skipped, and you can change or remove an answer at any time.

Your location

Your approximate location, only if you allow it. Your phone rounds it to about 1 km before it reaches us, and we never ask for your precise location. Without it, you choose your city yourself.

Other people see your city and a rounded distance, never where you are. In Travel mode you choose a city to appear in instead.

Legal basis: our contract with you, and your permission on the phone.

Likes, matches and messages

Who you like or pass on, your matches and your messages.

We use them to run Blum: to show your matches, deliver your messages and suggest profiles you’re more likely to like. Your messages are seen only by you and your match, unless one of you reports the other (see the next section).

When either of you unmatches or blocks the other, the conversation disappears for both of you. We keep a sealed copy for 30 days so that either of you can still report it. Nobody reads it unless one of you makes a report, and after 30 days it’s deleted.

Legal basis: our contract with you; for the sealed copy, our legitimate interest in keeping members safe.

Safety: reports and blocks

If you report someone, we keep your report and a copy of the latest messages between you, so a moderator can review it even after a block. Reports are confidential: the person you report isn’t told who reported them.

A profile reported by several different people is hidden automatically until a moderator has looked at it.

If we decide something about your account or content, we tell you in the app what we decided, why, and how to challenge it. If you report someone, we tell you whether we took action, but not what. We log every decision: who made it, when and why.

If we find child sexual abuse material, we report it to the police and, where the law requires it, to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) in the USA and Cybertip.ca in Canada. If we believe someone’s life or safety is at risk, we tell the authorities.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in keeping members safe, and our legal obligations, for example under the EU Digital Services Act.

Face verification

Verifying your profile is optional and needs your explicit consent. Your phone’s camera checks that you’re a real person, by asking you to turn your head and blink, and that you look like your photos.

The check runs on your phone and the camera images are thrown away immediately: we never store or send your selfie, video or any face data. We keep only that you’re verified and the date. You can remove the badge at any time in Settings → Privacy.

So we never collect, receive or store biometric identifiers or biometric information, and have none to keep or destroy. Before the check, we ask for your consent in writing: the “I agree” tick on the verification screen.

Verification never looks for a disability, dwarfism or any other trait.

Legal basis: your explicit consent (GDPR Article 9(2)(a)), because comparing your face with your photos uses biometric data.

Plans and payments

Purchases go through Google Play, which handles your payment details. We receive which plan or item you bought, when it renews or ends, and your purchase history, never your card details.

Legal basis: our contract with you, and our legal obligations, such as keeping tax records.

Notifications

With your permission, we send notifications about new matches, messages and likes. They never name anyone or quote a message, so your lock screen stays discreet. You choose which ones you get in Settings → Notifications, and you only get offers or news if you switch them on.

To reach your phone, we keep a code that identifies this copy of the app (a push token) and its language, and Google’s Firebase Cloud Messaging delivers the notification. We delete the code when you sign out or delete your account.

Legal basis: your consent.

Usage statistics and crash reports

To find where people get stuck, we count how many people complete each sign-up step, without any name or ID.

If you say yes (we ask once, and nothing is pre-ticked), we also collect usage statistics, such as swipes and which screens are used. They’re linked to a random code, not to your name, email or profile, and never include your messages, answers, health answer or location. You can turn this off at any time in Settings → Privacy, which also deletes what you shared.

If you also tick crash reports (a separate choice, never ticked for you), the app sends a technical report when something goes wrong: what failed in the app’s code, the app version, and the phone’s model and Android version. Reports go to Sentry, are stored in the EU and never include your name, email, messages, answers, photos or location. You can turn them off at any time in Settings → Privacy.

We use no third-party analytics or advertising tools.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in improving Blum, for the step counts; your consent, for usage statistics and crash reports.

When you connect to Blum

When your phone connects to Blum, your IP address and the time of the connection reach our servers, as with any app or website. Our hosting and database providers, and the companies they use, can see them too and keep them in their own records, to run their services and protect them from attacks.

Our own logs only record which feature was used, whether it worked and how long it took. They never contain your IP address, your name, your ID or anything you wrote.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in keeping Blum running and secure.

How matching works

To decide the order of the profiles you see, Blum combines how far away people are, their ages, shared hobbies and themes, your matching answers and the kinds of profiles you like. This is called profiling.

It only changes the order you see people in. It doesn’t decide who can see you: your choices and our rules do. No decision with legal or similarly significant effects on you is made by automated means alone.

We use Claude, an AI service from Anthropic, to help order the profiles you see, write the “why you might match” line and suggest first messages, in the language you use Blum in. It receives only what profiles already show (age, hobbies, conversation themes, bio, job and what someone is looking for), a few everyday habits from the matching questions, such as being an early bird or a night owl, and your app language. It never receives names, photos, locations, health answers, sexual orientation, or answers about children, smoking or drinking, and it only sees a message when someone taps Translate on it (see Translation).

A bio is whatever its author chose to write, so it can include things about their health. If yours does, that text reaches Claude with the rest of your profile, so leave it out of your bio if you’d rather it didn’t.

Anthropic processes this only to answer our requests, under its data processing agreement (our contract is with Anthropic Ireland). Its terms for business customers say it doesn’t use this data to train its models, and it deletes it within 30 days, unless it must keep it longer to enforce its usage policy or to comply with the law.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in suggesting people you’ll get on with, and, for anything in a bio that reveals health, the explicit consent its author gave when signing up. You can object to AI matchmaking (see Your rights).

Translation

When you tap Translate under someone’s bio, conversation theme or message, that text is sent to Claude to translate it into your app language. Only the text and the language are sent: never who wrote it, who you are, or anything else about either of you. Nothing is sent until you tap Translate.

To avoid translating the same text twice, a translation is kept on our server for at most a day, without any name or ID, and then deleted. Anthropic handles the text as described above and doesn’t use it to train its models.

What people write can include things about themselves, such as their health. It’s only translated because the person reading it asked, to understand what was shared with them.

Legal basis: our contract with you, to provide the translation you ask for, and, for health details in the text, the explicit consent its writer gave when signing up.

Who else receives your data

  • Other members see your profile, as described above.
  • Service providers that run parts of Blum for us, under contracts that oblige them to protect your data and use it only for Blum: our hosting and database providers, Anthropic (AI matchmaking and translation), Google (Sign in with Google, Google Play purchases and delivering notifications), Sentry (crash reports, only if you say yes), and the services that deliver sign-in emails.
  • Public authorities, when the law requires it or someone’s safety is at risk, including reports of child sexual abuse material.

We never sell your data or share it for advertising.

Data outside Europe

Blum’s server is in Paris, France, and its database in Frankfurt, Germany, but some providers are based outside Europe and may handle your data from there. When your data leaves Europe, each provider protects it as follows:

  • Our database provider, in Singapore: the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses.
  • Anthropic, in the United States: the Standard Contractual Clauses in its data processing agreement.
  • Google, in the United States: the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework.
  • Sentry, in the United States, which stores crash reports in the EU: the Standard Contractual Clauses in its data processing agreement.

For people in Switzerland, the matching safeguards apply: the Swiss–U.S. Data Privacy Framework, or the standard clauses approved there.

How long we keep data

  • Your account and profile: until you delete your account.
  • When you delete your account, your profile, photos, answers, likes, matches and messages are deleted straight away, and removed from backups within 30 days.
  • Reports and moderation decisions: 12 months after a case is closed. If a case may lead to legal proceedings, we keep what’s needed until they end.
  • Sealed copies of unmatched or blocked conversations: 30 days, unless one of you reports the other.
  • If an account is banned or suspended: a scrambled code made from its email, for as long as the ban or suspension lasts, so the same email can’t sign up again in the meantime. If an account is deleted while a report about it is open, we keep the code for 12 months, so nobody can avoid a review by deleting their account and signing up again. The code doesn’t show the email, it only lets us recognise it.
  • Purchase records: as long as tax law requires.
  • Usage statistics: until you turn them off or delete your account. The step counts contain no name or ID.
  • Crash reports: up to 90 days.

Your rights

  • See your data: Settings → Privacy → Download my data gives you a copy, in a file other apps can read.
  • Correct it: in Edit profile, or by asking us.
  • Delete it: Settings → Delete account. Your health answer can also be deleted on its own in Settings → Privacy.
  • Withdraw your consent at any time, for your health answer, orientation, face verification, usage statistics or notifications. This doesn’t affect what was done before.
  • Object to what we do based on our legitimate interests, including AI matchmaking, and ask us to limit the use of your data while a question is being sorted out.
  • Complain to the Portuguese data protection authority, the CNPD (www.cnpd.pt), or to the authority where you live, for example in Switzerland the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (www.edoeb.admin.ch).

To use any of these rights, write to blum.dating.app@gmail.com. We’ll answer within one month.

If you live in the USA

This part adds to the rest of this policy for people in the USA, including under the California Consumer Privacy Act and the similar laws of other states, where they apply to us.

In the categories these laws use, we collect: identifiers (your email address, or your name and email from Google); characteristics of protected classifications (your age and gender, and, only with your consent, your disability and health answer and your sexual orientation); commercial information (your plans and purchases); internet or other activity (your likes, matches and messages, and usage statistics and crash reports if you say yes); approximate geolocation (rounded to about 1 km, only if you allow it); your photos and what you write in your profile; and the order we suggest profiles in, which these laws count as an inference. We collect it from you and your phone, and from Google if you sign in with Google, and use it only for the purposes in this policy.

We use sensitive personal information, such as your health answer, your sexual orientation and your messages, only to provide the service you asked for, never to infer anything about you for other purposes.

We don’t sell your personal information and don’t share it for cross-context behavioral advertising, and never have. We don’t share it with anyone for their own direct marketing (California’s “Shine the Light” law). We don’t knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 18.

Our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy explains which health data we collect and share, and your rights over it, under Washington’s My Health My Data Act and similar state laws.

You can ask to know what personal information we have about you and get a copy, to correct it and to delete it (see Your rights). You can use an authorized agent; we may ask them for proof that you asked them to act for you, and ask you to confirm who you are. We won’t treat you differently for using your rights.

If we refuse a request, we’ll explain why. You can appeal by replying to our answer, and someone who wasn’t involved will look at it again within 45 days. If you’re still not satisfied, you can contact your state’s attorney general.

Blum doesn’t track you across other apps or websites, so “Do Not Track” signals change nothing for us, and we treat a Global Privacy Control signal as a request not to sell or share your data, which we never do anyway.

Your data is stored in the European Union (see Data outside Europe).

If you live in Canada

This part adds to the rest of this policy for people in Canada, under the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and provincial laws such as Quebec’s Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector.

The people in charge of the protection of personal information at Blum are Afonso Miguel Martins Delgado dos Santos and Duarte Miguel Bandeira Lisboa Bento. You can reach them at blum.dating.app@gmail.com.

We ask for your express consent before using sensitive information, such as your disability and health answer or your sexual orientation, as described above. You can withdraw your consent at any time, within the limits this policy explains.

Your data is stored in the European Union, and some providers handle it from the United States (see Who else receives your data and Data outside Europe). Before personal information of people in Quebec leaves Quebec, we assess the risks to your privacy and make sure it will be adequately protected, and our providers are bound by written contracts.

If our automatic check refuses one of your photos, that decision is made by automated means alone. You can ask us, at the address above, which information was used and why, and have a person review the decision.

You can ask to see your personal information, correct it, get a copy in a format other apps can read, or delete it (see Your rights). If you’re not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (www.priv.gc.ca) or, in Quebec, the Commission d’accès à l’information (www.cai.gouv.qc.ca).

If a security breach creates a real risk of significant harm to you, we’ll tell you and the authorities, as the law requires.

Only for adults

Blum is only for people aged 18 or over. You tell us your date of birth when you sign up, and we close any account we find belongs to someone younger.

Keeping your data safe

We encrypt your data on its way to and from Blum, limit access to the people who need it, and do the most sensitive checks on your phone. If a data breach puts you at risk, we’ll tell you and the authorities, as the law requires.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we’ll tell you in the app before the change applies.
